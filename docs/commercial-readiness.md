# Commercial readiness checklist

Audio MCP gives an agent local tools to produce music and sound effects for apps, games and video. The first commercial offer proposed here is a music pack for a freelance editor delivering a narrated 30-second ad and a 15-second cutdown. The editor buys one recognizable theme that fits both edits, leaves the voice clear and lands on the final brand frame. The pilot should measure how much editing that saves and whether the editor pays for it.

## What the generator can deliver

- [x] **Two arranged styles.** Velvet Keys suits a warm, low-key brief. Night Drive offers a steady electronic pulse for a product reveal. Both have a mastered stereo WAV, editable MIDI and optional drum, bass, keys and lead stems. The current demos still share recurring eight-bar harmony and recognizable synthesized drums. [Listen to both demos](../README.md#produce-a-music-cue) and [compare the scores and paid references](production-readiness.md#comparison-with-paid-stock-music).
- [x] **Exact lengths for one tested setting.** On 2026-11-23, the producer built from a clean snapshot on macOS arm64 with Node 24.21.0 and FFmpeg 8.1.2. It rendered Night Drive with seed 42 at 128 BPM through MCP stdio. Eight bars produced 15.000 s (−14.17 LUFS, −1.37 dBTP). Sixteen bars produced 30.000 s (−14.04 LUFS, −1.42 dBTP). In each run the master and four stems read back as non-silent, equal-length, 48 kHz, 24-bit stereo PCM. The MIDI contained the four named instrument tracks. [Tested generator revision](https://github.com/yapweijun1996/Audio-MCP/commit/8dce8683b4cbaf874302356843d299bb2d22dd34)

The tool accepts bars and tempo. In those two renders the theme began at bar 3 and bar 5 respectively. For a matching cutdown, edit the original performance. For a relaxed Velvet Keys brief, preserve its groove at 86 BPM and edit to picture. Build the narration mix from the pre-master stems and master each finished version.

Paid libraries illustrate the delivery format. PremiumBeat lists 15-, 30- and 60-second edits, loops and stems for *Easy Days*. Melody Loops offers an underscore for *Sunset Chill*. Demand for the Audio MCP pack still needs a paid trial. ## Before taking a paid order

- [ ] **Finish one pack against a timed brief.** Mark the narration, reveal and final logo on the timeline. Deliver a 30-second main mix, a 15-second edit with the same hook and cadence, and a 30-second underscore. Include the main mix's four aligned stems, MIDI and a file list with version, duration and BPM. Open the files in a fresh editing session. Check the ending against the requested frame and meter every finished mix against the brief’s loudness and true-peak limits.
- [ ] **Audition against picture.** Compare the cue with a rights-cleared stock track at matched music loudness under the same voice-over, with labels hidden and order varied. Listen on headphones and a phone speaker, then fold to mono. Note timestamps where the backing masks a word, a drum fill sounds identical on each pass, the lead feels mechanical, or the ending clicks or cuts off. Fix those moments and repeat playback. Record which version the editor would deliver and the corrective edit time.
- [ ] **Clear the offer.** Record the generator revision, seed, inputs and provenance of any added recordings. Define who may use the pack, for which client, media, territory and term, and how the terms cover paid ads, stems, resale and Content ID. Keep a copy of the agreed terms with the order. The arranged producer synthesizes its own sounds. Distributing software or sound banks has its own [distribution review](production-readiness.md#qualify-a-candidate-on-its-target-host).
- [ ] **Rehearse fulfillment.** Qualify the production host, render the brief, and replay the archived files after a restart. Put the price, delivery time, included revision and refund terms on a test invoice. Exercise payment in test mode, receipt, private download, replacement delivery and refund. Keep the accepted mix and terms under the order ID so a replacement delivers the version the buyer approved. [Host qualification](production-readiness.md#qualify-a-candidate-on-its-target-host) · [Rollback](production-readiness.md#rollback-and-sign-off)

## Decide whether to keep selling

Try **S$79 for one nonexclusive pack with one minor revision** as a pilot quote. The price and thresholds below are proposed decision rules. The repository records audio measurements and has no buyer results for this offer.

- [ ] **Test five active briefs.** Give five independent editors the same offer. Ask each to place the cue in an actual edit. Record the stock alternative, cue choice, minutes spent fixing the music, and reason for accepting or declining. A first target is three editors willing to deliver the cue with five minutes or less of corrective work.
- [ ] **Measure paid demand.** Look for two distinct buyers paying the quoted price. Record payments, refunds and actual production, revision and support time. A listening preference counts toward the music test. A settled order counts toward sales.
- [ ] **Check the return per order.** For example, S$79 less S$4 in fees, S$3 in render and delivery costs, and 30 minutes of work at S$48/hour leaves S$48 before acquisition and fixed costs. At 90 minutes the same order breaks even before those costs. Replace every estimate with the actual figures, including refunds and any per-order acquisition or licensing cost.
- [ ] **Expand after repeat use.** Look for a repeat order and positive contribution after variable costs before investing in a larger catalog or self-service checkout. If editors use the tracks but decline the quote, revisit the offer. If they repeatedly flag the same musical passage, prioritize that generator change and replay the affected briefs.
